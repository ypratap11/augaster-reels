## Website article (augaster.com/digest)

### Same winning strategy, different bet size, opposite outcome

**The setup.** Two traders use the same strategy: it wins 55% of the time, and wins and losses are the same size. Trader A risks 2% of the account per trade; Trader B risks 25%. Each starts with $10,000 and makes 100 trades. We simulated each 10,000 times.

**What happens.** The median A account ends at $11,972. The median B account ends at $5,103. B's account fell to half or less at some point in 80% of runs, versus 0.0% for A.

**Why.** Gains and losses multiply rather than add. A 25% loss needs a 33% gain to recover, so large bets let ordinary losing streaks do lasting damage, even with a real edge.

**What it means for you.** How much you risk per decision matters as much as how often you are right. Size positions so a normal losing streak cannot remove you from the game.

**Our take.** Survive first, then compound. This is education, not financial advice.
