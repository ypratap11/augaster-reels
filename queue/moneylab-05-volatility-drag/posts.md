## Website article (augaster.com/digest)

### Same average return, very different result: volatility drag

**The setup.** Investment A gains 5% every year. Investment B alternates: +30% one year, −20% the next. The simple average of B's returns is also 5% a year.

**What happens.** Starting from $10,000, after 20 years A is worth $26,533 and B is worth $14,802.

**Why.** A loss shrinks the amount that the next gain applies to. A 50% loss followed by a 50% gain turns $100 into $75. This gap between average return and compound return is called volatility drag. B's actual compound growth is 2.0% a year.

**What it means for you.** Two funds can show the same average return and leave you with very different amounts. Compare compound annual growth rates and drawdowns, not just averages.

**Our take.** Lower volatility is worth something even at the same average return. This is education, not financial advice.
