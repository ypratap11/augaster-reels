**What happened.** On October 21, 2026, Walmart announced the Walmart Agent Gateway, together with Agent Profiles and terms for AI agents. That is about a month before the November 21, 2026 roundup that presents it as news. Walmart's control over outside agents works through its published terms: agents that do not follow them will be blocked from Walmart's site.

**How it works.** The Agent Gateway is a channel for AI agents to shop on Walmart. It is meant to be faster and more reliable than agents using the website through a browser. It uses an MCP, which Walmart's post spells out as "model text protocol." Agent Profiles give each authorized AI agent its own profile inside the customer's Walmart account, so customers can see the agent's purchases separately. Under the terms, customers must approve agent purchases by default, and Walmart may require extra approvals even within an agent's spending limit. Customers can require approval for every purchase or let an agent buy within a limit they set. Walmart's examples are $50, $100 or $200, and these are examples, not fixed tiers. Agents do not receive customers' payment-card information and cannot add or change a customer's payment methods. The gateway is also meant to help agents shop for a customer's "usual" items without sharing personal information or purchase history with the agent. Walmart and Sam's Club agent shopping tools include the Agent Gateway, and Walmart already lists conversational commerce integrations with Muse, ChatGPT, Gemini and others.

**Availability and price.** The gateway starts as a beta for companies and developers. Access is limited, by invitation only, and may be suspended at any time. It is not a general launch. Agent Profiles are due to launch "in the next month" from the October 21, 2026 announcement, with no exact date given. Walmart says the prices an AI agent sees are the same as those a human sees on Walmart.com, and applicable offers and fees are shown before purchase. Separately, Walmart's Sparky usage in the U.S. was up 129% year over year, per Walmart's Q3 FY27 earnings page. The roundup says it "more than doubled" in the third quarter of fiscal 2027.

**What it means for small businesses.** AI agents are becoming a real kind of shopper, and large retailers are now writing the rules for how they buy. The catch is that this is a limited, invitation-only beta that can be suspended at any time, so it is not something most small businesses can plug into today. It is, however, a clear preview of the controls shoppers may come to expect: default approvals, spending limits, separate agent records and no sharing of card details.

**Our take.** Learn how these approval and spending-limit models work now, even if you never sell through Walmart. If AI agents start buying from your shop, customers may expect the same kind of clear permissions and visibility. Treat this as a design reference, not a to-do item.
