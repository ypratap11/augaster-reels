**What happened.** On 17 November 2026, Suno announced and launched Visuals, a new tool that creates lyric videos, album art and video clips from songs, including a clip re-imagined from a user's photo. The announcement was written by Jack Brody, Suno's Chief Product Officer. It follows Suno's Speech (beta) tool, launched on October 1, 2026, for spoken audio such as poems and meditations.

**How it works.** Users drag a song from their Suno library into the Visuals tab. The video model follows the song's structure, picks up on the lyrics and lip-syncs to them. Clips are up to 15 seconds long and can be extended, edited and re-prompted with text. You can start from Suno's original characters or upload your own original image, and exports are available at different durations and in 9:16, 1:1 and 16:9. Visuals is powered by H3 Move, a model based on MiniMax's H3 that Suno optimized with added protections and safeguards. Suno says it added text and visual filters to block uploads and prompts that try to depict specific artists, public figures, copyrighted works and IP. Generated videos and images are checked against Community Guidelines, using the same filters plus third-party tools, before the user sees them. Suno continues to work with Audible Magic, ACRCloud and Musixmatch to block uploads containing copyrighted audio, and embedded watermarks are applied to Visuals before they can be shared off-platform.

**Availability and price.** Visuals launched on web on 17 November, and the iOS and Android versions were reported to follow in the coming weeks. Visuals credits are separate from song credits, and credit packs can be bought at suno.com/pricing/visuals. The credit cost of a Visual depends on factors such as clip duration and quality. Users choose between 'fast' and 'standard' quality, and fast costs fewer credits. Pro and Premier subscribers get a limited number of Visual credits to try for a limited time. Any user can buy credit packs, and purchased credits don't expire.

**What it means for small businesses.** If you already use Suno to make music for ads, intros or social posts, Visuals offers a quick way to turn a track into a short clip in a social-friendly shape. The catch is cost and rights. Credits are separate from song credits, so your video budget is its own line item, and shared clips carry embedded watermarks. There is also a legal backdrop to be aware of: MiniMax is being sued for copyright infringement by Disney, Universal and Warner Bros Discovery, and a US federal judge declined to dismiss the case, which centres on its Hailuo AI video platform. Whether that affects you is a question to weigh before using clips in paid campaigns.

**Our take.** Treat Visuals as an experiment first. Make one short clip on fast quality, see whether the lip-sync and style suit your brand, and only then consider buying credit packs.
