**What happened.** TikTok has started rolling out Likeness Detection globally. It is an opt-in tool that helps eligible creators find and flag AI-generated content on the platform that may use their likeness without consent. The rollout is gradual, not instant. It is available to eligible creators and public figures, including Government, Politician, or Political Party Accounts. TikTok announced it at its safety and innovation event in London, and positioned it against AI-generated disinformation ahead of a wave of European elections next year. One source gives the year as 2027, and another names Italy, France and Greece. Francesca Scapolo, TikTok's Government Relations Lead for European Elections, said the tool would help politicians and government identify potentially AI-generated content shared potentially without their consent.

TikTok also launched a second tool, a public transparency checker. Anyone can upload an image or video to see whether it was generated or edited with TikTok's AI creation features, even without a TikTok account.

**How it works.** Likeness Detection works in three steps. Eligible creators verify their ID with a third-party partner. They are notified of content and accounts that may use their likeness and can review them in the tool. They can then report content directly to TikTok's safety teams. Content that violates TikTok's community guidelines against harmful, misleading or nonconsensual depiction of private individuals will be removed. TikTok says the tool is designed to minimise data collection and preserve privacy, and that it does not keep ID documents.

The public checker looks for C2PA Content Credentials and for VeraDura, TikTok's proprietary invisible watermark, which TikTok says is more durable and harder to remove. It detects content made or edited with TikTok's own AI tools. It is not a general-purpose AI detector.

TikTok also shared some figures. It says its AI labelling tools and technologies have helped it label more than 5 billion AI-generated videos to date, a cumulative total. It says it became the first video platform to adopt C2PA Content Credentials, in 2024, and automatically adds them to AI-generated content made with its tools. It says its in-app AI literacy hub, launched in July, has helped more than 4 million people learn to spot AI-generated content. TikTok is a member of the C2PA Steering Committee. TikTok also says it removes more than 99% of content that breaks its rules before anyone reports it, and that anyone, whether or not they use TikTok, can report nonconsensual AI-generated content through its privacy reporting form. TikTok is owned by China-based ByteDance.

**What it means for small businesses.** Likeness Detection is limited to eligible creators and public figures, so many small businesses and their owners may not qualify. The public checker is open to everyone, but it only recognises content made or edited with TikTok's own AI tools. A result that shows nothing does not prove a video is authentic. The privacy reporting form is open to anyone, which may be useful if someone uses AI to imitate you or your staff.

**Our take.** If someone fakes you or your brand, report it quickly and keep a record of what you found. Use the checker as one signal among several, not as proof. If you are a creator or public-facing owner, find out whether you are eligible for Likeness Detection as it rolls out.
