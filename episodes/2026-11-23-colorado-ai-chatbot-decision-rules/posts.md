## Website article (augaster.com/digest)

### Colorado adopts AI rules for chatbots and automated decisions: what small businesses should know

**What happened.** The Colorado Department of Law adopted its Automated Decision-Making Technology (ADMT) and Chatbot Safety Act rules on November 19, 2026, after an October 26 public hearing. The rules are not yet final or in force. The Department has requested a formal opinion on them from the Attorney General. After that, the rules and the opinion are filed with the Secretary of State, and the rules become final twenty days after publication. Earlier steps were the filing of proposed rules on August 11, 2026 and an interim draft on October 6, 2026.

**How it works.** The Chatbot Safety Act (HB 26-1263) requires chatbot operators to estimate user age, disclose that users are talking to AI, safeguard teens against sexually explicit content and simulated emotional dependence, and offer privacy and account tools for minors. It also requires suicide and self-harm response protocols and bars presenting chatbot output as equivalent to licensed professional services. Operators must submit an annual report to the Attorney General that includes additional metrics on the efficacy and reliability of safeguards, though the statute does not detail the report's content. On the automated-decision side, SB 26-189 repeals and reenacts the 2024 Colorado AI law (SB 24-205) with new requirements for ADMT used in consequential decisions. Deployers must acknowledge a request for meaningful human review within ten days and complete the review within forty-five days. There is one extension of up to forty-five days in limited circumstances, and none where the decision affects food, shelter or required medical care. One report says chatbot age checks can't rely on ID alone and that AI-assisted denials need a thirty-day explanation. Those two claims rest on a single source, and we could not check them against the adopted rules text.

**Availability and price.** Both SB 26-189 and HB 26-1263 take effect January 1, 2027, and the Attorney General must adopt implementing rules before that date. SB 26-189 was signed in May 2026. Sources disagree on when HB 26-1263 was signed, so we are not stating a date.

**What it means for small businesses.** If you run a customer-facing chatbot, expect duties around disclosure, age estimation and teen safeguards. If AI helps you make consequential decisions about people, expect human-review and explanation requirements. The catch is that the rules are adopted but not final, and the two headline claims are not yet confirmed against the primary text, so details may change.

**Our take.** List where you use chatbots and AI in decisions about customers or applicants, and note who would handle a human-review request. Then wait for the final rule text before making any big changes.
