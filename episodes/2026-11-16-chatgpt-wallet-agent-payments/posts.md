## Website article (augaster.com/digest)

### OpenAI's ChatGPT Wallet lets AI agents buy things for you

**What happened.** OpenAI began rolling out ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, November 13, 2026. The wallet lets OpenAI's dots agents, which are its AI agents, complete purchases on a user's behalf. OpenAI describes it as the first wallet that lets agents pay without access to actual card details and requires a security check for every purchase. That "first" is OpenAI's own claim and is not independently verified.

**How it works.** Users add their card once and do not re-enter payment details for each purchase. Before every purchase, the user reviews the items, the total and the selected card, then approves with a security check. That check is a biometric such as Face ID or a fingerprint scan, and each approval creates a record of the purchase the user authorized. Payment uses a payment token, a substitute for the real card number, security code and expiry, and the selected card is charged. Users keep their existing card rewards and protections for eligible disputes.

**Availability and price.** The initial launch is through dots, for ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US who are aged 18 or older. The launch is limited so OpenAI can learn from real-world use and improve compatibility and reliability with businesses, banks and payment networks before expanding.

**What it means for small businesses.** The wallet is designed to work with existing commerce infrastructure, so existing checkout pages should work without a Wallet-specific integration. OpenAI advises merchants to allow verified ChatGPT cloud-browser requests using Web Bot Auth. Businesses can also build plugins that let agents handle product discovery, availability, carts and checkout, and OpenAI has released a Commerce Partner Playbook and other tools and guidance for supporting customers who use agents. The catch is that sites blocking AI agents may see Wallet purchases fail. OpenAI also plans to let users set spending limits, purchase categories or time periods and pre-approve purchases, so agents can act more autonomously. That is a future plan, not available now.

**Our take.** This is early, and access is narrow, so there's no need to rebuild anything today. But if you sell online, it's worth checking whether your site blocks AI agents and deciding on purpose whether verified agents should be let in. If agent-driven shopping grows, being easy for agents to buy from could become a quiet advantage.
